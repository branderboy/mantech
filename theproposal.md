## A Note on Terms

A **Mission Assistant** is a supervised, governed AI capability built for one defined mission function. It is not an autonomous agent and it is not a replacement for a federal employee or contractor. It carries repetitive execution under human direction, flags uncertainty, and hands every judgment call back to the people accountable for the mission. Throughout this document, "Mission Assistant" always means *human-supervised augmentation* — never independent decision-making.

## Why Agencies Can Trust It

The honest concern with AI is simple:

*What if it confidently does the wrong thing and my name is on it?*

Mission Workforce Infrastructure is built so that cannot happen quietly.

Every Mission Assistant operates inside four controls.

**1. It Knows When To Stop**

When an assistant is not sure, it does not guess. It stops, flags the issue, and routes the task to a human for review. Human judgment stays active where the stakes are highest.

**2. Everything Leaves A Trail**

Every action, recommendation, source, and decision path is logged and traceable. When a result is questioned months later in an audit, budget review, oversight hearing, or internal challenge, the rationale already exists.

**3. It Watches Itself**

Assistants are continuously monitored for accuracy, reliability, quality, and drift. Problems are surfaced early, before they become mission risks.

**4. Humans Stay In Command**

People approve, reject, validate, and decide. Assistants execute. Accountability never leaves the chain of command.

The bottom line: this is not autonomous AI making mission decisions. It is a governed workforce layer that does the heavy lifting, shows its work, and hands judgment back to trusted humans.
